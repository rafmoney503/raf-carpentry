Most people who could draw a wardrobe in 3D never start. Not because it's hard, but because the first ten minutes are. You open the program, the screen is full of tools, and nothing tells you what to do with your hand on the mouse.

Build It in 3D starts there.

## Where it came from

The lessons began on the CabinetOS site as a course for cabinet makers learning SketchUp. While I was writing them, it became clear the people who needed them most weren't only cabinet makers. They were anyone who had never drawn anything in 3D: young learners, older learners, people changing trade.

So the lessons now have a home of their own, at [builditin3d.com](https://builditin3d.com), and they're free.

## It starts before any drawing tool

The first lessons are about the basics everything else sits on:

- using the mouse to move round a model
- zooming in and out without getting lost
- drawing a flat shape
- pushing and pulling it into something solid

Each lesson is short and ends with an exercise, so you do it rather than watch it. A box comes first, then a cabinet.

## For any age

I want a grandparent and a ten-year-old to be able to sit at the same computer and both get it. That means no jargon in the early lessons, one thing at a time, and nothing that assumes you already know where the buttons are.

## Where it leads

The lessons grow step by step into SketchUp for cabinet makers: drawing units at real sizes, the way I draw every job for Raf Carpentry. From there it's one step to the next tool in the chain, [CabinetOS for SketchUp](/#design), which draws the units for you.

If you know someone who has always wanted to draw something in 3D, send them the link. The first lesson takes a few minutes.
