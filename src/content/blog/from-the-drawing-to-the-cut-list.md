Drawing a wardrobe and making one are two different jobs. The drawing shows the client what they're getting. The making needs every part: its size, its board, its edging, and how the parts fit on a 2440 × 1220 sheet with as little waste as possible.

For years those were two separate pieces of work, and the gap between them is where mistakes live. CabinetOS started there: a cut-list mistake in a spreadsheet cost me money on a job. So I built a tool where the cut list comes from the design itself, and the two can't disagree.

## Design: units that size themselves

CabinetOS for SketchUp is an extension for drawing wardrobes and cabinets inside SketchUp with the same maths as CabinetOS. You place a unit and set its size. Every part is its own component, and the parts adjust with the unit: change the width and the top, the base and the shelves all follow.

Because each part is real, cutting tools that read SketchUp parts can work with the drawing too.

## Make: the cut list from the design

[CabinetOS](https://www.cabinetos.co.uk), the Cabinet Operating System, takes the design and works out the rest:

- every part, with its size, listed for the cut
- the parts nested on 2440 × 1220 boards
- drawings to print and take to the bench
- a 3D link your client can open on their phone

On the CabinetOS demo job, 85 parts fit on 10 boards with 9% waste.

## Why it matters on a real job

When the drawing and the cut list are the same thing, a change the client asks for on Thursday is in the cut list on Thursday. Nobody copies sizes from one screen to another, so nobody copies one wrong.
