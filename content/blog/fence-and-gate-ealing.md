Out with the old fence and trellis. In with a close-board fence across the back of the patio, and a gate that matches it.

![The old trellis above the brick wall, new boards leaning against it](/images/blog/fence-and-gate-ealing/old.jpg) ![A new post with the first boards on, the mitre saw set up beside it](/images/blog/fence-and-gate-ealing/posts.jpg)

Posts first, then rails between them. String lines run from end to end to keep the posts in one straight row, and the rails sit in metal brackets on the posts.

![Posts and rails across the garden, with string lines along them](/images/blog/fence-and-gate-ealing/rails.jpg#narrow)

Then the boards. Close-board means upright boards, each one overlapping the next, so there is nothing to see through.

The gate sits near the end of the run, by the brick pier. It is ledged and braced, hung on T-hinges with a bolt and a latch. The braces run up from the hinge side, which is what stops a gate dropping at the latch end over the years. On the boarded side, the boards carry on over the top of the gate, so it reads as a doorway in the fence.

![The gate open, with the boarding carried over the top of the opening](/images/blog/fence-and-gate-ealing/gate.jpg) ![Fence and gate across the back of the patio](/images/blog/fence-and-gate-ealing/patio.jpg)

Fence and gate are finished in a warm brown stain.
