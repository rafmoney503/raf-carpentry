The doors are the part everyone sees. The part of this job that mattered most is above the ceiling, and nobody will see it again.

## What made it hard

A sliding door hangs from a track, and glass is heavy. That track needs something solid to fix into along its whole length.

Here, above the ceiling there were metal joists and insulation. The joists cross the line of the doors, so a track fixed up into them would only find something solid every so often. Between those points there was nothing for it to hold on to.

## Opening the ceiling

So I opened the ceiling in a strip along the line of the doors.

![The ceiling opened along the line of the doors, with metal joists and insulation inside](/images/blog/glass-sliding-doors-haringey/ceiling.jpg) ![Timber fixed between the metal joists](/images/blog/glass-sliding-doors-haringey/timber.jpg)

Then timber went in between the joists, a piece in every gap, the whole length of the run. Once that was done, the track had solid timber to hang from wherever it needed it.

![The whole run of timber in place, ready to board over](/images/blog/glass-sliding-doors-haringey/run.jpg#narrow)

After that I closed the ceiling up again, before the doors went in.

## The doors

Four glass panels in thin black frames, each with two bars across it. They slide to close off the room without losing the light, and you can still see straight through to the window at the far end.

![The doors from the hallway side](/images/blog/glass-sliding-doors-haringey/doors.jpg) ![The doors looking through to the living room](/images/blog/glass-sliding-doors-haringey/room.jpg)

From the room, none of the timber shows. That is how it should be.
