Brick walls, a fence along one side and sliding doors at the house end. This small courtyard in Camden Town is now decked from the doors to the planters at the far end. Here is the order it went down in.

## 1. Membrane down

Weed membrane over the whole garden first, so weeds do not come up between the boards later.

![Weed membrane laid across the courtyard, with the plants pushed into one corner](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/membrane.jpg) ![The timber frame laid out over the membrane, with cross pieces between the joists](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/frame.jpg)

## 2. A frame, packed up level

The frame is a set of joists with short cross pieces between them, set out across the garden and levelled. The cross pieces keep the joists upright and stop them twisting. Short blocks of timber under the joists bring the whole frame up to the line. A level frame is what makes a level deck, because the boards follow whatever is under them.

![Joists sitting on timber blocks at the far end of the garden](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/blocks.jpg#narrow)

The frame goes right up to the doors.

## 3. A black strip on the joists

Before the boards, a black strip went along the top of the joists. It stops water sitting on the timber under each board.

## 4. Boards down

The deck boards were stacked down the side of the house, ready to go.

![The deck boards stacked along the side wall](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/boards.jpg#slim)

The first boards went down at the doors, and the rest worked out from there across the frame. The doors are where you step out and where you see the deck from, so that is where the first straight line belongs.

The mitre saw sat on a sheet of ply laid over the part of the frame not yet boarded. Every cut was a step away from where the board was going.

![The first boards fixed at the doors, with the black strip on the joists beyond](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/first-boards.jpg) ![Boards going down, the mitre saw on a sheet of ply over the frame](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/saw.jpg)

## 5. The edges

The boards are cut in round the walls. A border board runs along the edge, mitred where it turns a corner, and the deck stops neatly at the planters.

![The border board along the edge, stopping at the planters](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/border.jpg) ![The finished deck from the doors end, running to the planters](/images/blog/decking-camden-town/finished.jpg)
