Come in through the front door of this house in Palmers Green and you are in a small porch, with the hall door straight ahead. There is a wall either side. Both are now storage, mostly for shoes.

![Looking through the porch to the hall door: tall cupboard on the left, shoe cupboard and bench on the right](/images/blog/shoe-storage-palmers-green/porch.jpg#narrow)

## What went in

- **A tall cupboard on the left.** Two panelled doors, and behind one of them a column of shallow shelves for shoes.
- **A narrow shoe cupboard on the right,** next to the hall door. One panelled door, with shoe shelves set close together, one above another.
- **A bench** beside it, by the window, to sit on while you take your shoes off. Its front is panelled like the doors.
- **Vertical boards on the wall** behind the bench.

![The tall cupboard with its two panelled doors](/images/blog/shoe-storage-palmers-green/cupboard.jpg) ![The bench, the boarded wall and the narrow shoe cupboard](/images/blog/shoe-storage-palmers-green/bench.jpg)

## Behind the doors

Every cupboard door has three panels and hangs on concealed hinges. Each door in the photos below has four of them. I set the hinge hole centres 21 mm in from the door edge and cut each hole with a hinge jig set to depth, so no hole goes too deep.

![The narrow shoe cupboard open, shallow shoe shelves one above another](/images/blog/shoe-storage-palmers-green/shoe-shelves.jpg) ![The tall cupboard with one door open and more shoe shelves inside](/images/blog/shoe-storage-palmers-green/tall-open.jpg)

The cupboards are 18 mm board, backs included. All of it is MDF, left bare and ready for the decorator.
