A round table sits in the bay of this kitchen in Kennington. A straight bench would have left awkward gaps, so the seat follows the curve of the table, and everything under the seat is storage.

## Drawn twice before cutting

I sketched the curve by hand first, then drew the whole seat in SketchUp. The model let me settle the curve and the panel layout before a single board was cut.

![The curved seat drawn in SketchUp](/images/blog/curved-storage-seat-kennington/1.jpg)

## Building a curve in MDF

The curve was drawn out full size on a sheet of MDF. From that line I cut matching curved frames, then built the storage boxes between them. Before any panels went on, I set the whole shape on the ground and checked it, so I knew it would sit right in the bay.

![Curved MDF frames cut out on the bench](/images/blog/curved-storage-seat-kennington/2.jpg) ![Storage boxes built between the curved frames](/images/blog/curved-storage-seat-kennington/3.jpg)

The front is panelled and follows the curve all the way round. The lids on top lift up, so the whole length of the seat is storage.

![The panelled front of the curved seat](/images/blog/curved-storage-seat-kennington/4.jpg) ![The finished seat next to the round table](/images/blog/curved-storage-seat-kennington/5.jpg)

## The tools that did the work

Straight cuts came off the Festool track saw on its rail. The curves were cut with the DeWalt jigsaw, and the Makita trim router and the DeWalt sander took care of the edges.
