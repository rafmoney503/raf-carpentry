The reception desk is the first thing visitors see when they walk into this office in Brentwood. It had to look the part, and it had to work for whoever sits behind it all day.

## What it is

- L-shaped, so it greets people coming in and still gives the staff side plenty of room
- A raised counter on the visitor side
- A slatted front
- Shelves under the counter on the staff side
- A matching unit to one side, with a slatted panel of its own

## Built on plinths

The desk sits on plinth frames. I built those first and set them out on the floor, packed level. The line of the desk was marked on the carpet with tape, and the carcasses went on following that line.

Getting the plinths right is most of the job. If the base is level and in the right place, everything that sits on it goes together cleanly.

![Plinth frames set out along a tape line](/images/blog/reception-desk-brentwood/plinths.jpg) ![The front run going on](/images/blog/reception-desk-brentwood/front-run.jpg)

## Painted before the final fit

The L-shaped top runs across both sides of the desk. The panels and slats were painted before the final fit. Painting slats once they are fixed means getting a brush into every gap, and the edges between parts never look as clean.

![The slatted front](/images/blog/reception-desk-brentwood/slats.jpg)

*The slatted front, painted before it went on.*

![The L-shaped top](/images/blog/reception-desk-brentwood/top.jpg#narrow)

## The staff side

On the staff side there are shelves under the counter, so the desk top can stay clear. The side unit matches the desk, slats and all.
