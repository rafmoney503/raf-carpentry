This kitchen in Crouch End had its base units and worktops in place before I started. My part was four things:

1. **V-groove panelling** above the worktops, and at dado height round the dining side, finished there with skirting.
2. **The cooker hood**, boxed in to match the panelling.
3. **A wall cupboard** by the window, panelled down its side.
4. **A tall unit** at the end of the run, with panelled doors.

## The panelling

Battens went on the wall first. The coloured horseshoes are plastic packers. Old walls are rarely flat, so the packers bring the battens out to one line, and the panels then sit flat on top of them.

Beside the boiler, the pipes were measured up so the panelling could box them in neatly. Then the V-groove sheets went up behind the worktop, cut round the sockets and switches.

![Battens and plastic packers on the wall above the worktop](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/battens.jpg) ![Tape measure across the pipes beside the boiler](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/pipes.jpg) ![The first V-groove sheet up behind the sink, the track saw and rail on the worktop](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/panels.jpg)

## The hood

The old extractor stayed where it was. A frame went round it first, set out with laser lines on the wall, and then it was boxed in with MDF: a sloped canopy, a ledge with timber mouldings, and a straight chimney up to the ceiling. The extractor's lights still shine out underneath.

![The frame built round the old extractor](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/hood-frame.jpg) ![The hood boxed in, with the laser on the wall by the fridge](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/hood.jpg)

## The cupboard and the tall unit

The wall cupboard sits by the window, panelled down the side to match the walls. The tall unit stands where the worktop stops. Their doors have the same upright grooves as the doors on the base units below. That ties the new pieces to the kitchen that was already there.

![The wall cupboard by the window, with one door on](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/cupboard.jpg) ![The tall unit doors, made up and leaning on the dado panelling](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/doors.jpg) ![The tall unit at the end of the run](/images/blog/kitchen-panelling-crouch-end/tall-unit.jpg)

In these photos it is all still bare MDF with timber mouldings, ready for the decorator.
